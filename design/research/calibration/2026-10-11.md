## Reading (by hand, 2026-10-11)

What the two sets are, honestly: the good side is 4 reels the creator chose
as references plus 14 YouTube vlogs / travel films with ≥100k views (5 of
19 links failed to download); popularity stands in for "well cut", and the
set mixes 9:16 shorts with 16:9 cinematic films. The bad side is 18 VibeCut
exports of **one** shoot, and 11 of them are Remix iterations of nearly the
same board, 3 are single-shot talking-head cuts and 2 are 11-second drafts.
In kind that is about five independent edits, not eighteen. So every
number here is *better than a guess*, not yet a calibration in the CLAUDE.md
sense; the next record needs rejected edits from other shoots and other
people.

Moved in `grammar.py`, citing this record:

- **rhythm_cv** (separates, AUC 0.78): WARN edge 0.35 → **0.6**. Good edits
  breathe (CV 0.81); VibeCut's beat-quantised remixes march (0.56). Set
  short of the 0.70 split because the bad set's evenness is partly the
  remix quantiser's doing.
- **cut_on_motion** (weak, right direction): OK edge 0.55 → **0.45**, BAD
  0.40 → **0.30**. The old edge warned on most *good* edits (median 0.47).
- **jl_cuts** (weak, right direction): WARN 0.45 → **0.6**; BAD stays 0.7.

Left alone, and why:

- **duplicates** separates (good 0, bad 1 pair) and the rule already warns
  on any pair: nothing to move.
- **continuity, first_cut, long_shot, loudness**: no signal on these sets.
  The first-cut and long-shot guesses (5 s, 12 s) sit far from either
  set's values, which means they rarely fire on anything; a larger bad set
  is needed before trusting or dropping them.
- **first_speech** reads 0.01–0.3 s on everything: `speech_mask` is
  level-based and hears music as voice. Calibrating it is pointless until
  the voice reading (sound.py) feeds it.
