Notes: WHAT IS PUBLIC, AND HOW COMPLETE THIS IS - Basic (free) version: 20 cards in 11 groups. Group names translated: Persistent layer (chapter-bar, pin-board, caption-track); Evidence (stat-proof); Data metrics (ring-metric, odometer, rank-bars, growth-curve); Contrast/trade-off (versus-card); Key lines/quotes (quote-lockup, punch-pill); Steps/process (step-timeline, checklist); Tutorial annotation (term-card, ui-callout); Text entrances (blur-text, type-shift); Person anchoring (entity-chips); Scene · camera (focus-card); Scene · B-roll (terminal-3d). All 20 are in the items. - Pro (¥200 launch price, list ¥399): 102 cards in 15 categories. The 15 are the 11 Basic groups plus four Pro-only kinds of expression, which the site maps onto the classical four-part arc (opening / development / turn / conclusion): Information structure (section-header titles that say 'what's next'), Transitions (let paragraphs connect and carry the rhythm on), Emotional peak (push the key line and its emotion up), and Atmosphere noise-floor (an atmosphere layer for texture). ARRANGEMENT RULES WORTH REUSING (behaviour, from the docs) - Plan segments first, then pick cards: chapters, then 10-30 s segments each carrying exactly one point, then elements timed to sentences. At most one signature main card per segment, plus a pin board for the smaller points. - Put a hook card in the first 0-5 s. Aim for a new action (a new card, or the next item inside one) every 2-4 s. - Density: the default preferences say 8-12 cards/min; the operations guide targets 10-16. At most 2 cards on screen at once by default (the guide allows up to 4). Stagger entrances by at least 0.5 s. Never two cards of the same kind in a row. - Durations: short cards 3-5 s, content cards 5-12 s, camera cards 8-15 s, persistent cards for the whole video or chapter. Full-screen cards (focus-card) never share the screen. - Timing inside a card is computed from speech: step ≈ span in seconds × 1000 ÷ number of items, minus 10%. - Layout: cards avoid the person in the centre and must not cover the face. ### Styling per vibe (Overlay Studio cards)

- **Chapter Progress Bar:** HUD-tech (OS native): thin dark translucent strip, small bold labels, tick dividers. Editorial-minimal: a hairline with small caps. Cozy-handmade: hand-lettered paper tabs. Cinematic: very thin, low opacity. OS recommends it only for videos longer than 3 min, so skip it on short-vertical. On 9:16 four names don't fit across 1080 px; use the compact '01 / 04' chapter-counter tile the website shows instead (a numeral between two hairlines).
- **Key-Point Pin Board:** HUD: dark glass panel with an accent subtitle. Cozy-handmade: sticky notes or push-pins, slight random rotation, paper texture. Editorial: numbered list with hairlines. Playful-pop: pins bounce with overshoot. On vertical formats put it in the top third, keep it to 3 items or fewer, and never beside the face. Vlog uses: packing list, 'what went wrong today'.
- **Bilingual Caption Track:** Its fixed position is a consistency rule in itself, which matches VibeCut's 'fixed subtitle position'. HUD: bold white, key word in accent blue, small English underneath. Cinematic: lighter weight, no glow, smaller. Playful: the key word scale-pops. Editorial: key word underlined instead of recoloured. On 9:16, lift it above the platform's UI safe zone.
- **Stat Proof (giant counting number):** HUD: huge numerals with a letter-spaced mono kicker (the demo uses 'LOSSLESS EXPORT'). Editorial: serif numerals with a hairline rule. Playful-pop: overshoot bounce on landing. Vlog uses: '14 km walked', '¥38 dinner', 'day 3 of 10'. The number must come from the creator, never be invented.
- **Ring Metric / Data Ring:** HUD-glass is native. Clean-minimal: 2 px stroke, no glow. Playful: thick rounded stroke with a bounce at the end. Cinematic: thin arc, desaturated. Vlog uses: 'phone at 12%', '80% of the trip it rained'. The FX/014 variant with the label inside the ring is the more compact choice for 9:16.
- **Odometer Counter:** HUD: mono digits in small dark tiles. Retro/cozy: split-flap board, cream on charcoal. Playful: the digits over-rotate and settle. Vlog uses: steps, kilometres, countdown to departure.
- **Ranking Bars:** HUD: thin bars on a dark plate, the value at each bar's end. Editorial: hairline bars in one ink colour. Playful: chunky rounded bars that bounce. Vlog uses: ranking the cafés or hikes of the trip. On 9:16 keep it to 4 rows or fewer.
- **Growth Curve:** HUD: glowing line with a fading gradient. Editorial: plain black line with dot markers. Cozy: hand-drawn wobbly line in marker. Vlog uses: altitude over a hike, spend over a trip (real numbers only).
- **Versus Card:** HUD: two dark glass panes with a split accent seam. Playful-pop: the panels slam together and the loser gets a strike-through. Editorial: two columns with a thin rule. Vlog uses: 'expectation vs reality', 'hostel vs hotel'. On 9:16, stack the sides top and bottom instead of left and right.
- **Quote Lockup:** HUD: frosted dark glass with an accent quote mark. Editorial: large serif italic on paper. Cinematic: centred white text over dimmed footage. Vlog uses: a sign, a local's saying, a friend's message (the words must be real).
- **Punch Pill:** HUD: dark pill with an accent outline. Playful-pop: saturated filled pill with a wobble. Editorial: black pill with white text. Cozy: hand-cut paper label. Works very well on short-vertical as a hook in the first 5 s.
- **Step Timeline (vertical outline):** HUD: vertical line with nodes that light up. Editorial: numbered serif list. Cozy: a dotted trail with hand-drawn dots. Vlog uses: the route (airport → ferry → village).
- **Checklist:** HUD: square checkboxes that fill with the accent. Cozy: hand-drawn ticks. Playful: ticks bounce and the item strikes through. Vlog uses: a day's to-do list or packing list.
- **Term Card:** HUD: mono English on top, bold term, muted definition. Editorial: dictionary-entry styling. Vlog uses: local words ('onsen: public hot spring bath').
- **UI Callout (ring highlight):** HUD: thin accent ellipse with a leader line. Playful: marker-drawn circle that 'draws itself'. Cozy: chalk circle. Pinning it to a moving object needs tracking, which VibeCut doesn't have; keep it to static shots.
- **Blur-In Text:** Suits cinematic and cozy vibes best. HUD: white with a soft glow and an accent key word. Cinematic: thin weight, long stagger, no glow. Playful: faster stagger with a small scale-up.
- **Type Shift (typographic flow):** Editorial-minimal is its natural home (big/small type contrast). HUD: bold sans with a mono small line. Cinematic: an end-card look. Good as a vlog closing card.
- **Entity Name Chips:** HUD: white and black chips with a mono ID line. Editorial: a news-style strap. Cozy: luggage-tag look. Vlog uses: introducing a friend, a guesthouse host, a restaurant. It needs face-safe placement, and VibeCut has no face detection yet.
- **Focus Camera Card:** This changes the video itself (scale and position), so the footage part needs ffmpeg and the points can be a canvas overlay. Cozy/editorial: cream background, rounded frame. HUD: dark background with an accent ring. On 9:16: the video goes into the top half and the points into the bottom half. OS never auto-places these cards; it only suggests them per chapter for the creator to decide.
- **3D Terminal Typewriter:** Tech/HUD only. In a vlog, the equivalent is a phone chat or ticket screen typed out, but OS itself says to put long chat text on a real screenshot or recording rather than retyping it. Matting the person on top would need segmentation, which is out of VibeCut scope.

- **Evidence Quote Card (paper):** Its natural home is cozy-handmade or editorial (paper, tilt, offset shadow). HUD version: a dark card with a mono header. Vlog uses: a museum plaque, a review snippet, a friend's text (real words only).
- **Trend Bars with Delta:** HUD: dark-red translucent fills with a mono delta. Clean: flat grey plus one accent bar. Playful: bars bounce. It compresses well into 9:16, so it's a good data card for short-vertical.
- **Section Title / Chapter Opener:** Editorial-minimal: this is its native look. HUD: mono kicker in the accent colour. Cozy: handwritten 'Day 3' with a drawn underline. For vlogs it replaces the chapter bar on short and vertical formats ('Day 2 · Kyoto').

The HUD is a canvas overlay. Cinematic: drop the REC HUD and keep the moves. On 9:16 the split should stack top and bottom. It needs resolution headroom: a ×1.6 push on 1080p source softens.
- **Warp Title (star-trail opener):** HUD/sci-fi native. In VibeCut, put it over darkened or blurred real footage rather than an empty background. Cozy version: slow drifting dust or bokeh instead of streaks. Cinematic: streaks at low opacity. It is a Pro 'transition' example.
- **Stair Bars Glass Panel (with ring counter):** HUD glass with a blue accent. Clean: a white panel with a thin border. On 9:16 stack the ring above the panel. Only real numbers.
- **Converge (info constellation):** HUD with purple glow. Cozy: hand-drawn bubbles joined by doodle lines. Vlog uses: 'what made this trip' (food / people / weather / slowness) around a centre photo cut from a real frame. On 9:16 keep the six pills within a circle over the upper half.
- **Demo Tour (tilted frame push):** The video must change (scale plus perspective or rotation); the frame border and glint can be an overlay. Cinematic: skip the tilt and do a slow push. Uses only the creator's frames. The presenter photo on the website is OS's own asset — don't reuse it.
- **Accent-Bar Lower Third:** Works in HUD, editorial and cinematic styles (a thin rule instead of a plate). Vlog uses: place or name captions ('Kyoto · Day 2'). On 9:16 it sits above the caption band.
- **Burst Halo (emotional peak):** Playful-pop and HUD native. Cinematic: no word, just a subtle halo pulse plus a push-in. Cozy: hand-drawn burst lines. Use at most once or twice a video (VibeCut's taste line: where the emotion goes belongs to the creator).
- **Energy Meter (LIVE):** HUD/gaming vibe. For VibeCut, drive the bars from the real audio level of the clip (the 16 kHz track from media.load()) so the meter is measured rather than decorative; drop the fake percentage unless it's tied to something real.
- **Character Assemble:** Playful-pop: bouncy snap. Editorial: a tighter stagger, no rotation. Cinematic: a slow blur-in per character with no scatter. A good end card for a vlog.
- **Light Sweep Transition:** HUD: cool white glint. Cinematic or cozy: warm amber light-leak (as an ffmpeg blend over real frames). Playful: a hard-edged colour wipe. It could be the default Pro 'transition' feel; whether it's a card or just demo-player chrome is unclear.
- **Spoken-Already Dim (recede, don't exit):** Works in any style. It lets VibeCut keep 2-3 cards on screen without clutter. On 9:16 prefer 'fade' so the layout doesn't reflow within the narrow width.
- **Atmosphere Noise-Floor Layer (Pro, only named):** For VibeCut this maps naturally onto grain, vignette, light-leak or gentle-haze layers over real frames (ffmpeg or canvas), switched per aesthetic: film grain for cinematic, paper texture for cozy, scanlines for HUD. ### Styling per vibe (vlog elements)

- **Place stamp (location title):** cinematic: no pin; widely tracked uppercase in thin sans or serif, a hairline rule, lat/long in small mono, slow 0.8 s fade. cozy: rounded pin, handwritten or rounded font on a cream pill, warm tones. clean: small system-sans, simple pin glyph, white text with a soft shadow, no box. film/analog: typewriter mono or an ink-stamp look, amber text, 1 px jitter. playful: bouncing red pin with a thick outline, bright sticker pill tilted 3-5 degrees. hype: huge condensed caps slammed in 0.12 s with a 4 px shake and a flash frame.
- **Route map (A to B travel line):** cinematic: dark, desaturated vector map, a thin glowing line, slow push, globe establishing shot. cozy: beige paper map with a hand-drawn dashed line, a doodled plane and soft pins. clean: white or grey minimal map, a blue line, small labels. film/analog: sepia paper, compass rose, stamp marks, a dotted path. playful: a cartoon vehicle, bouncing pins, saturated colours. hype: a fast 3D globe spin, snap zooms, motion blur, a neon line.
- **Film-camera date stamp:** film/analog: the native form, a seven-segment amber digit with a slight bloom in the bottom-right corner. cozy: warmer, smaller, softer glow. clean: small white mono at 60% opacity, no glow. cinematic: usually left out, or replaced by a thin sans timecode in a corner. playful: a pastel pink or lime colour. hype: rarely used; if it is, add an RGB split or glitch.
- **Camcorder REC viewfinder HUD:** film/analog: full VCR look with chroma bleed and noise bands (on the ffmpeg side). playful/Y2K: pastel HUD, sparkle stickers. clean: only the corner brackets and a small REC dot. cozy: warm-tinted text, no noise. cinematic: usually not used. hype: glitch bursts on the beat. Round the timecode to whole seconds and the blink to 1 Hz so the exporter can still reuse identical frames.

**Recommendation:** Split motion by what it needs. Anything that moves or recolours the footage itself goes to ffmpeg. Anything that is a graphic on top goes to the Canvas. Overlay Studio's own model is the same: pre-tuned motion cards rendered as transparent alpha layers stacked over the edit. Its typography, data, emotional-beat and chapter cards map to VibeCut's Canvas overlay. Its camera operations (push-in, zoom-out return) map to the shot stage.

SHOT STAGE (export.py _shot, reading the original 4K file):
1. Convert HLG/Dolby Vision to SDR Rec.709 first (scale_vt color_*=bt709, then transpose=2 after hwdownload). Every grade and LUT assumes SDR, and today HLG tags go straight into 8-bit output. Verify framing before adopting.
2. All camera moves use one primitive: perspective with sense=source, eval=frame, cubic, driven by frame index `in` (which starts at 1). That covers slow Ken Burns (0.025 px jitter), the eased punch-in (0.03 px from the intended curve) and sway. The instant emphasis punch is a plain crop from the 4K frame: free, with 67% more detail than cropping the 1080 intermediate. The punch anchor must follow the subject; a centre punch cut the face in half on the real clip.
3. Shake: crop with sin() offsets. Speed ramps: piecewise setpts, clamped to 0.5x or faster for 60p sources and 1x for 30p. Freeze: loop or tpad. Ramps and freezes change frame counts, so timeline.py must own the output durations.
4. Speed: render about 3 shots in parallel. Measured 2.7x to 4.1x realtime for the baseline, and 1.16x to 1.79x with moves and grade.

JOIN / FINISH:
5. Transitions need both shots' pixels, so they cannot be stream-copied. When transitions exist, run xfade in one pass: 7.8x realtime for 11 joins, and a 0.5 s xfade costs nothing measurable over a hard cut. Better, fold it into the finish pass, which already re-encodes whenever overlays exist; that avoids a third encode generation.
6. Ship these: fade (6-10 frames), fadeblack (chapters), fadewhite (energy), smooth*, cover*/reveal*, hblur, and fadegrays for nostalgic looks. Build eased pushes and the whip-pan from hstack + a moving crop + avgblur resized per frame via sendcmd. It looks like a real whip and costs the same as a cut.
7. The global look also belongs in the finish pass: one LUT per vibe (lut3d, tetrahedral), plus the procedural light leak and the thresholded glow. Both of those need true screen blends on footage pixels, and screen blends must run in RGB, or they put a magenta cast on the whole frame. Short rgbashift glitch windows go here too.

CANVAS OVERLAY (overlayDraw.ts, identical in preview and export):
8. All Overlay Studio-style motion cards: kinetic type, lower thirds, data rings, chapter openers, emphasis stickers.
9. White flash hits (a few alpha-white frames), letterbox bars, the vignette (one static radial-alpha PNG, exactly equivalent to ffmpeg's vignette) and any static texture.
10. Keep per-frame Canvas animation to short entrances and exits. Each distinct frame costs about 8.7 ms of PNG decode in ffmpeg, and a full-length animated layer halves finish speed (6.97x to 3.1x).

TOO SLOW OR TOO UGLY TO SHIP:
- Film grain on VideoToolbox: 0-7% of it survives, even at 24 Mb/s or at 2 px grain size. x264 only keeps it at crf 14 and 76 Mb/s.
- xfade zoomin: nearest-neighbour blocks and a grey veil.
- zoompan, and scale(eval=frame)+crop zooms: stair-stepping jitter, and a stale-anchor bug.
- minterpolate: 0.13x realtime.
- xfade custom expressions: 0.2x realtime; hstack+crop does the same thing.
- tmix with enable: outputs corrupt green frames.
- Blend modes run in YUV.
- The curves vintage preset.
- The dated xfade family: hard wipes, diag*, iris circle/rect, radial clock, random dissolve, slices, wind, squeeze, and pixelize except for a retro vibe.

Vibe presets can then bundle one LUT, one transition family, one move style and one card style. For example, warm-film = warm LUT + leak + hblur/fadewhite + slow Ken Burns. Clean-modern = no texture + eased cover/reveal pushes + punch-ins. Energetic = whip + flash + short RGB split + punch-ins.

## 4. Gaps the completeness critic found, and what the fill agents returned

### Nobody has measured what continuously animated overlays cost in the export. Every card is tagged canvas-overlay, but the only export measurement (web/README.md: 6:44 edit, 8 overlays, 139 s on an M5) used near-static titles. frameKey() in overlayDraw.ts dedupes identical frames, so any card whose hold keeps moving produces one full-size RGBA PNG per frame: Energy Meter bars, Burst Halo breathing rings, Warp streaks, the chapter-bar progress fill, odometer rolls and blur-ins. export.py sets MAX_FRAMES = 20000, which caps continuous animation at about 11.1 min at 30 fps. A YouTube long vlog with any persistent animated layer would hit that cap. Also unchecked: the cost and browser support of ctx.filter blur (used by Blur-In, Warp Title, Big-Type and Character Assemble), and the fact that Canvas 2D can only do affine transforms, with no perspective (Demo Tour tilt, 3D terminal).

I built the benchmark in `/tmp/vc-motion-bench` and ran it in Chrome 154 and Safari 26.4 on a MacBook Pro M5. Nothing in the repo was modified. The per-frame PNG path works for cards, but three things decide the render hints: Safari doesn't blur at all, a 15-min vlog with one living layer hits the frame cap, and there is a frame-timing bug in `_overlay_input` that affects every animated overlay.

**Caveats**
- An unrelated CPU benchmark (`bench_cpu_gpu.py`, another project) held the 4 performance cores for much of the session (load average 5–43), so browser wall times are ranges. The median encode per PNG stayed steady; the spikes are stalls.
- Someone else edited `export.py` (17:40) and `transport.ts` during the run. The parts measured here (`MAX_FRAMES`, `_overlay_input`, the finishing filter) are unchanged.

**How it was run**
- `server.py` mirrors `api_export_frame` and calls the real `export.save_frame`. `bench.html` copies `renderSchedule`: OffscreenCanvas → `convertToBlob('image/png')` → POST, at most 4 uploads in flight, one PNG per distinct key.
- Chrome ran in an isolated profile: `Google Chrome --user-data-dir=/tmp/vc-motion-bench/chrome-profile "http://127.0.0.1:8877/?browser=chrome"`. Safari: `open -a Safari "http://127.0.0.1:8877/?browser=safari"`. That Safari tab is still open.
- Composite timing: `finish_only.py h|v REPS RUNS label:sched:<jobdir>` over real `IMG_4492.MOV`, using the real `_shot`, `_overlay_input`, `_venc` and `_ff`.

**Results per 60 s timeline, 30 fps**

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a) card, 12/min (288) | 6.7–57 (6.4–7.9) | 16–17 (14–15) | 57–61 / 50 | 2.2–2.8 / 45–47 ms | 6.8 s |

- **Draw is not the cost.** It takes 0.1 ms or less, or 0.6–2.1 ms when forced to finish. Blur adds about 1.5 ms in Chrome.
- **Finishing doesn't depend on the overlay.** One static PNG also takes 6.8 s per minute. Under contention everything took 9.5–14.5 s.
- **The frame cap works as designed.** Frame 19999 returns 200; frame 20000 returns 400 "frame number out of range".

**Extrapolations**
- **60 s vertical short, 12 cards per minute:**
  - Cards with still holds: 288 PNGs, 2–2.5 s in Chrome and 4.5 s in Safari, well under the cap.
  - Every card with a moving hold: 1800 PNGs, 13–42 s Chrome, 27–90 s Safari.
  - Finishing adds about 7 s, and rendering the shots adds 20–43 s (measured on real 4K HEVC).
- **15-min YouTube vlog with one layer that moves every frame:**
  - That is 27,000 distinct PNGs, **so the export fails at 11:06.7**.
  - Before failing, the browser has already spent 2.4–7.8 min (Chrome) or 4.9–11 min (Safari).
  - It would also write 1.3 GB (equaliser-sized frames) to 12.5 GB (full-frame layer) of PNGs.
  - Without the cap it would take roughly 9–20 min in total.
- **A looping layer is free.** With the equaliser's 1800 PNGs cycled across 27,000 schedule entries, finishing took 158 s, against 170 s for one static PNG. The cap only counts distinct frames.

**Bug in production export (found, not fixed)**
- The ffconcat input's timestamps snap to 25 fps (time_base 1/25), so overlay frames land on a 40 ms grid.
- A test clip with a frame counter burned into each overlay frame showed the wrong overlay on 600 of 1800 frames. One source frame in six is never shown and two display a frame late. Continuous motion judders, and titles can start or end up to one frame off.
- Adding `option framerate 30` after each `file` line, tested in scratch only, gave 1800 of 1800 exact frames at the same cost.

**Browser capabilities**
- **`ctx.filter`:** works in Chrome on canvas, OffscreenCanvas and in a worker. Safari 26.4 has the property but ignores it everywhere, so blur-in exports sharp — I checked the frames visually.
- **Perspective:** neither browser supports it. A 3D matrix passed to `setTransform` drops its perspective term (`m34` → 0), and both edges of a rotated rectangle stay 120 px tall.
- **WebCodecs with alpha:** not supported for any codec. Chrome: "Alpha encoding is not currently supported." Safari: "Alpha keep is not supported."
- **Colour and matte packed side by side in one H.264 WebCodecs stream:**
  - Works in both browsers and is frame-exact: 7.0 ms/frame in Safari, 7–14 ms in Chrome.
  - Much smaller: about 6 MB per minute for the equaliser (PNG: about 120 MB) and 16–22 MB for the sweep (PNG: about 1.1 GB).
  - No frame cap, but finishing takes 8.9–11 s per minute.
  - It needs a range conversion plus `blend=all_mode=divide` to undo premultiplied alpha. `overlay alpha=premultiplied` darkened translucent frames in this ffmpeg build.
  - Matches the PNG path at 39–46 dB on the equaliser and blur.
  - One of the ten Chrome runs hung with no error, so it would need a watchdog. It also can't skip unchanged frames.
- **Other transports:** raw RGBA is 8.3 MB per frame, so no. WebP is slower in Chrome and Safari returns PNG anyway.

**Recommendation**
1. **Must keep pixel-still holds and only change the frame key when pixels change:** title, lower-third, caption, name and date cards, odometer rolls (still between changes), and blur-in, Warp Title and Character Assemble (at most 0.6 s of motion). The chapter-bar fill must step in whole pixels.
2. **Persistent layers that move** (Energy Meter, Burst Halo, Warp streaks) must loop over at most about 2 s. A layer that follows the music and can't loop moves to ffmpeg or the packed-matte stream.
3. **Full-frame atmosphere** (sweeps, leaks, flashes) renders in ffmpeg. One pre-rendered band moved by an `overlay` x(t) expression took 6.8 s per minute with no browser work.
4. **Blur family:** needs a detect-and-fallback, or Chrome-only export, before it ships.
5. **Perspective** (Demo Tour tilt, 3D terminal): Canvas 2D can't do it. ffmpeg `perspective` ran at 6.7 s per minute but smeared the edges; WebGL is untested.

The scratch folder takes 24 GB. `/tmp/vc-motion-bench/view/` has the comparison frames, and the result logs are in `/tmp/vc-motion-bench/results/`.

### Transitions, one of Overlay Studio Pro's four categories, is barely covered. The only items are the Light Sweep (which may just be the demo player's own chrome) and the Warp Title. export.py implements only dissolve, dip-to-black and flash, plus open/close fades, and it falls back to a hard cut when a shot has no handles. The Homebrew ffmpeg's xfade has 58 built-in transitions (verified: wipes, slides, smooth*, circleopen/close, zoomin, hblur, pixelize, squeeze, cover/reveal, and more), and dblur, rgbashift, perlin, blend, tmix and lagfun are all present. Nobody has matched vlog transitions to these, worked out which need handles, or checked how often real footage has handles.

**Vlog transitions built only from the creator's own footage: catalogue plus measurements on the real clips (export.py unchanged)**

**What Overlay Studio does (from its page only)**

**Catalogue** (frames at 30 fps. "Handles" = footage past the cut. Durations are my proposals unless cited.)
1. **Hard cut / cut on motion**: default, every vibe, 0f, no handles. The cut is the most common transition; the others are used "selectively… to convey a tone" ([Film transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_transition)).
2. **Whip pan**: travel/energetic, 6–10f, handles needed. Build: `xfade=slideleft` plus `dblur angle=0` (radius 30, then 90 over the middle half). A whip hides the cut in blur ([Whip pan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whip_pan)), and blur runs along the motion ([Motion blur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_blur)). Sound: whoosh ([freesound](https://freesound.org/search/?q=whoosh+transition)). Direction: follow the camera move. Left/right in 16:9. In 9:16, use `slideup` + `angle=90` when the shot tilts.
3. **Zoom-through**: hype/city, 8–12f, handles needed. `xfade=zoomin` alone zooms into A and then fades B in; at the cut the frame is a flat smear of A (I checked the rendered frames). 4. **Light leak / film burn**: nostalgic/film, 12–24f, **no handles**. 5. **Flash (white)**: upbeat/beach, 4–8f, no handles (export.py already does this). Sound: shutter on the beat.
6. **Dip to colour**: calm/chapter end, 12–30f, no handles. Uses `fade color=` with a colour other than black, a "wash" ([StudioBinder](https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/types-of-editing-transitions-in-film/)). Sound: let the music tail play out.
7. **Match-cut dissolve**: calm/time passing, 24–48f ([StudioBinder](https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/types-of-editing-transitions-in-film/)), handles needed, because a dissolve overlaps both shots ([Dissolve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolve_(filmmaking))). 8. **Glitch / RGB split**: tech/night, 3–6f, no handles. Put `rgbashift` on A's last 3 frames and B's first 3 ([Glitch art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glitch_art)). Sound: glitch hit ([freesound](https://freesound.org/search/?q=glitch+transition)).

12. **J cut**: B's sound starts 6–15f early ([J cut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J_cut)). Needs audio handles on B's head only.
13. **L cut**: A's sound carries 6–30f over B ([L cut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L_cut)). Needs audio handles on A's tail only.

**Beat rule** (a guess until calibrated)
- Use `music.beat_grid()`. If it returns nothing, snap to shot cuts instead; if there are none, leave timing free.
- The hit frame is onset + t50 (onset + t90 for slams; the onset itself for exits). Snap it to `round(beat*30)/30`, but only if that moves the cue 3 frames or fewer away from its words.
- Tolerance at 30 fps: the visual hit may land up to 2 frames before the beat or 1 frame after it. This follows the ITU-R BT.1359 asymmetry (sound arriving before the picture is noticed at about 45 ms, after it at about 125 ms).
- Stagger = beat ÷ n, taking n from the vibe's allowed divisions, closest to its base stagger. Round each onset to a frame independently so errors don't add up.
- If settle is longer than one beat, shorten D (to no less than 0.6×). End each hold on a beat, lasting at least the larger of min hold and 2 beats. Put the sound effect's attack on the beat.

### Atmosphere ('noise floor') is one of Overlay Studio Pro's four categories, but it is only named. Nothing was described or prototyped: grain, vignette, halation/bloom, a drifting light leak, gate weave, dust, haze, HUD scanlines, or paper texture for the cozy look. It also cannot be a canvas overlay. Per-frame noise PNGs don't compress, and they would exhaust the frame budget within minutes.

**Atmosphere layer: ffmpeg-only recipes and measured cost**

I prototyped and measured all five recipes on one real clip: IMG_4476, 30 s of 4K60 HLG, graded with the real `look-subtle` LUT. The 16:9 version is a 16:9 crop of that same clip, not a separate landscape shot. Masters use h264_videotoolbox at 12M, the export's final rate for both sizes. Platform copies use libx264 medium: 2.5M for 9:16, 8M for 16:9. Only the creator's frames are processed.

The 2.5 Mbps Douyin/Xiaohongshu figure comes from the brief. I couldn't source it because the web-search budget was used up. YouTube's own recommendation for 1080p SDR at 24–30 fps is 8 Mbps H.264 ([YouTube](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1722171)). Overlay Studio names only "氛围底噪 — 为画面增加氛围层" (an atmosphere layer) and says nothing about how it works ([overlaystudio.cn](https://www.overlaystudio.cn/)). No Overlay Studio code was opened.

**Where it goes:** inside `_fit` in `web/backend/export.py`, in this order: scale → fades → lut3d → **atmosphere** → pad → bars.
- **After the LUT:** the look from `cutgrammar/grade.py` never re-tints the leak or changes the grain's strength.
- **Before pad:** letterbox and pillarbox bars stay pure black.
- **Before the caption overlay:** words stay clean.
- **Per shot:** grain gets a different seed per shot. The leak gets `t0` = the shot's start in the edit, so its drift doesn't jump at cuts.
- **Dips to black:** the shot's fade must also go on the small leak image, or the leak glows on a black dip. Grain in overlay mode already disappears in black.
- **Known limits:** grain dims to about 71% in the middle of a dissolve. With captions on, the grain also goes through a second encode (24M intermediate, then 12M final), which I didn't measure.

**Recipes** (full chains are in `atmosphere_bench.py`; filter docs at [ffmpeg-filters](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html))
- **Cinematic:** gray layer at ½ resolution, `noise=c0s=10:c0f=t`, bicubic upscale, `blend=c0_mode=overlay` on brightness only. Grain is about 2 px and strongest in midtones; real film grain also scales with brightness ([Norkin](https://norkin.org/research/film_grain/index.html)).
  - *Twos* variant: the same grain refreshed at 15 Hz.
  - *Fine* variant: 1 px grain at c0s=8.
- **Cozy:** `vignette=angle=0.5`, then a warm leak, then grain at ⅓ resolution with c0s=7.
  - The leak is one brightness expression on a 36×64 image. It drifts on 13 s and 9 s cycles and brightens and dims every 4.7 s.
  - It is tinted amber with `colorchannelmixer`, then applied with screen on brightness and grainmerge on colour, at 0.5 opacity.
- **HUD:** `rgbashift` by ±2 px (9:16) or ±3 px (16:9), plus soft scanlines every 6 px, 18% deep, applied with multiply. The scanline image is rendered once and looped.
- **Editorial:** only `vignette=angle=0.35`.
- **Playful:** a highlight glow. At ¼ resolution, keep only brightness above 175, blur with `gblur=sigma=7`, then screen at 0.45.

**Measured, 9:16 | 16:9**
- **Output size on the Mac path:** unchanged for every recipe, 45.2–45.6 MB per 30 s (12.1 Mbps). The encoder holds a fixed bitrate, so texture costs picture quality instead of file size. The Mac master alone already loses about half the grain (designed strength 3.8 → 1.7–2.1).
- **Size on the non-Mac path** (libx264 CRF 18, Mbps; no texture = 17.4 | 8.9):
  - cinematic 41.7 | 35.8
  - fine 45.9 | 41.7
  - twos 32.3 | 25.8
  - cozy 30.6 | 24.8
  - HUD 9.0 | 4.8
  - editorial 16.2 | 8.2
  - playful 17.4 | 8.8
- **Encode time:** on a quiet machine, wall time is about 0 s per minute of output, except cozy at +2.7 s. The hardware encoder is the bottleneck.
  - Extra CPU-seconds per minute of output: cinematic 7.6, fine 9.0, twos 6.1, cozy 22, HUD 22 (5 with `chromashift` instead of `rgbashift`), editorial 7, playful 10.
  - For scale, decoding and scaling the 4K source costs about 250 CPU-s per minute.
- **Grain actually reaching viewers** after the platform re-encode, as a share of the designed grain:
  - cinematic 26% | 29%
  - fine 19% | 26%
  - twos 28% | 37%
  - cozy 0% | 22%
- **Keyframe pulse** (grain on the platform's keyframes ÷ grain on other frames): cinematic 1.7 | 2.0, fine 1.7 | 3.3, twos 1.5 | 1.5, cozy 5.9 | 3.4. Above ~1.3, the grain visibly throbs every time a keyframe lands.
- **Blockiness:** the 8-px block-edge ratio is 1.05–1.07 everywhere, the same as with no texture. The grain doesn't turn into blocks; it gets smeared away and then pulses back at keyframes. This matches why AV1 synthesises grain at the decoder instead of encoding it ([AV1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AV1)).
- **Other textures kept after re-encode:**
  - scanlines 98% | 100%, pulse 1.01
  - chroma shift 69% | 91% (colour is stored at half resolution: [4:2:0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chroma_subsampling))
  - leak and vignette 98–100%
  - glow 98–99%
- **Platform quantiser (average QP):** no texture 29.3 | 20.4 → cinematic 31.2 | 25.5, twos 31.5 | 25.6.
- **Match to master (SSIM):** no texture 0.955 | 0.987 → cinematic 0.928 | 0.963.

**Verdict**
- **These survive:** vignette, the warm leak and the glow (all very smooth), plus the static scanlines. They cost nothing in bitrate; scanlines even lower it. Ship them.
- **Per-frame grain doesn't survive** the 9:16 platforms: about a quarter of it arrives, and it throbs at every keyframe.
  - Cinematic: use grain on twos. It survives best, pulses least and is the cheapest. Treat it as a 16:9 / YouTube feature; on vertical it reads only as softness.
  - Cozy: drop the grain on 9:16, where it is invisible except as a keyframe flicker. The leak and vignette carry the look.
  - Never use 1 px grain; it is worst on every measure.
- **Non-Mac path:** libx264 CRF grows 2–4.7× with grain. Add a `-maxrate` cap before grain ships there.
- **HUD:** switch `rgbashift` to `chromashift=crh=-1:cbh=1` (about ¼ the CPU). I measured only its cost, not how it looks after re-encode.
- **Not built:** gate weave (it re-samples the whole frame), dust (random specks, the worst case for encoders, like grain), and paper texture (static, so it should survive like the scanlines, but untested).

### The studies cover emotional peaks only as overlay cards (Burst Halo, Energy Meter). The peak moves vlogs actually use change the footage itself: freeze frame, speed ramp or slow motion into the moment, beat-synced zoom punches, camera shake, flash frame, stutter/echo, a colour pop (desaturate, then restore) and a letterbox slam. export.py has only a linear punch-in (zoompan) and must keep every shot frame-exact. Three questions were never investigated: how to do speed ramps without breaking the frame-count contract or the audio, which source frame rates allow real slow motion, and whether optical-flow interpolation (minterpolate) for slow motion from 30 fps footage breaks the 'no generative video' non-goal, since it creates frames nobody filmed.

- **Scope.** Overlay Studio's "Emotional Peaks" and "Camera Movement" cards sit on top of the video as transparent layers [1]. The 8 moves below change the creator's own frames instead, and each one pairs with a card. Prototype: `/tmp/vc_peaks/proto.py`, which reuses `_fit`/`_venc` from `/Users/barbiezhu/VibeCut/web/backend/export.py`.
- **Beat caveat.** `beat_grid()` returns beats but no bar position, and its tempo choice prefers the fastest near-tied pulse, so it can return double time (`cutgrammar/music.py:253-256,291-297`). A downbeat is beat 1 of the bar [6]. Until bar phase is estimated, "downbeat" below means a beat the creator confirms.

**Catalogue** (filter chain [2] · frames at 30 fps · beat · vibe · card pairing)
1. **Freeze** [7]: `loop=loop=L:size=1:start=F,setpts=N/30/TB`, with the source shortened by L frames. Audio uses `atrim`+`apad=pad_dur`+`concat`, so the sound resumes in sync. 18–30 frames. Freeze on the downbeat and resume on the next beat. Playful or documentary vibe. A name or place card lands on the held frame.
2. **Speed ramp**: a piecewise `setpts` in closed form (log-eased 1×→0.5×→1×) placed before `fps=30`. Audio is split in three: the ramp window gets `atempo=0.570,volume=0.25` and plays at 1× outside it. 60–120 frames. Slowest point on the downbeat. Cinematic or dreamy vibe. A quiet caption or quote inside the slow span.
3. **Zoom punch**: `zoompan=z='1+0.12*env(in)'` with a 2-frame attack and a 5-frame decay (or hold to the cut). 2+6–10 frames. The peak should land on the beat frame, so start one frame early (the prototype peaked at F+1). Hype vibe. A big-word card on the same frame.
4. **Shake**: 5% overscan (`scale=1134:2016`), then `crop=1080:1920:x='27+A*exp(-(t-t0)/τ)*sin(2*PI*11*(t-t0))':y=…`. Crop x/y are evaluated per frame. 8–12 frames, starting on the kick. Hype or sport vibe. An impact card.
5. **Flash**: `eq=brightness='0.7*exp(-(t-t0)/0.04)*gte(t,t0)':eval=frame`. 2–4 frames on the beat; it can hide a cut. Upbeat vibe. The card enters under the flash.
6. **Stutter/echo**: `lagfun=decay=0.9:enable=…`, or `split`→`tmix`→`overlay=enable=…`. A true stutter is `loop=loop=2:size=4` (adds 8 frames, so shorten the source). 6–12 frames across one beat. Retro or playful vibe. Trails on a kinetic word card.
7. **Colour pop**: `hue=s='if(lt(t,t0),0.08,min(1,0.08+(t-t0)/0.33))'`. Colour comes back over 10 frames, starting on the downbeat. Nostalgic vibe. The card's colour arrives with the footage's colour.
8. **Letterbox slam**: stepped `drawbox` pairs using `enable='eq(n,F)'` (bar height 55%→108%→100%). 3 frames in on the downbeat, then hold for the key line. Cinematic or moody vibe. A quote card sits between the bars. In 9:16, 420 px bars leave about a 1:1 window. `_bar()` currently letterboxes horizontal frames only.

- The same ramp on a 30 fps clip (`IMG_4407`) keeps 120/120 frames but repeats 23 of them. The hold drops to 15 unique fps and judders.
- Flash, colour pop and letterbox also rendered 90/90.
- **Problem found:** `tmix` with a timeline `enable` dropped 2 frames (88/90, a timestamp gap from 0.93 to 1.0 s), which breaks the frame-exact contract. `lagfun`, or `split`+`overlay`, gave 90/90.
- **Contract rule:** `item["frames"]` stays the timeline length and the move works out the source span (freeze uses n−L frames). Audio is cut at the same source times as the picture, then `apad` and `-t` trim it to exact length.

**Source frame rates** (85 index entries, which are 43 unique files, checked with ffprobe)
- **39 of 43 files were filmed at 60 fps** (36 at 60/1, 3 at 59.94). That is 491 of 517 seconds. They can be slowed to 0.5× without making up frames.
- One of them, `IMG_4475`, averages 57.8 fps, so it has a variable frame rate.
- 4 files are 30 fps (`IMG_4407`, `IMG_4408`, `IMG_4512`, `IMG_4513`). None are 120 or 240 fps.
- On a 30 fps timeline, 60 fps footage allows 0.5× at most. 0.25× needs 120 fps, which iPhone records only as Slo-mo (1080p at 120/240 fps) [3]. Filming at a higher rate is true slow motion; stretching footage afterwards needs fabricated frames [4].

**Open decision for the product owner: minterpolate**
- minterpolate calculates in-between frames [2], and motion interpolation can briefly tear or glitch [5]. Those are frames nobody filmed, which arguably crosses the "no generative video" line.
- Cost on `IMG_4407`, for 1.2 s at 0.5×:
  - `mci`: 4.4 s (3.6× real time)
  - `aobmc`+`bidir`+`vsbmc`: 10.4 s (8.7×)
  - `blend`: 0.45 s (a cross-dissolve, no invented motion)
- **Options** (not chosen here):
  - (A) Slow motion only on sources of 60 fps or more; 30 fps clips get freeze or punch instead.
  - (B) Allow minterpolate as an opt-in per shot, labelled "frames estimated".
  - (C) Allow `blend` only.
